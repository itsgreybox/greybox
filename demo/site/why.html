How it works

A real first look at what your legacy system actually does

Free, self-serve, no engagement to sign. Here's the actual method, what you get, and where it fits next to Swimm and OpenRewrite — both real, mature tools, checked directly, not guessed at.

The problem

📉Legacy estates accumulate risk quietly

Unsupported patterns, undocumented constants, and fewer people left who remember why something works the way it does.

🧩Decisions get blocked by the unknown

A modernization plan looks straightforward until it hits a dependency, or a business rule, nobody mapped.

💸The first look is usually the expensive part

Consultants charge $15K-$180K just to tell you what a system does, before any actual modernization work starts.

Three layers, working together

1️⃣Deterministic — ground truth first

Real parsing (AST for Python/Java/JavaScript) maps dependencies, undocumented constants, silent failure points, and flagged comments. Zero AI involved at this stage.

2️⃣AI — explains what was found

Only after the facts are extracted does AI get involved — its job is explaining specific findings clearly, not guessing what the code does from scratch.

3️⃣You — validate and decide

Unlike a service engagement, there's no SME between you and the output. The confidence score tells you exactly where you still need a human who knows the system.

Why doesn't a smarter AI model just replace this?

Someone who works in this space pushed back on me directly: "For traditional code, AI tools have gotten so good at understanding it that it's hard to compete against them directly. It becomes a game of providing the proper context, so results match what's needed."

That's the actual bet I'm making with how I built this. I'm not trying to out-understand code with a smarter model than Claude or GPT already are. greybox pulls out real structural facts first — dependency graph, undocumented constants, silent failure points, flagged comments — deterministically, with zero AI involved. Only then does the AI get that context, so its job shrinks from "figure out what's going on" to "explain these specific findings clearly." As models get better, that only makes the explanation better — the fact-extraction step is the part that doesn't get replaced by a smarter model on its own.

What greybox can't do

Same conversation, pushed further: "The challenge with the context part is that it's often proprietary in nature. There's some aspects you can solve, but oftentimes it truly will require someone with at least cursory understanding of the systems in question."

He's right, and I don't think that part is solvable by any tool. If the real reason something works a certain way only exists in someone's head, or a client contract from a decade ago that was never written down anywhere, no amount of code analysis gets that back. greybox doesn't recover lost context. What it does is narrower: it tells you exactly where you still need a person who remembers, instead of that person having to read every file to find out. Less "solves the problem," more "spends that person's limited time and memory where it actually matters."

Structured, sortable, diffable output for dashboards or CI checks, or a readable report you can send someone.

🔒Secret redaction

Hardcoded API keys, AWS keys, passwords, and DB connection strings stripped before anything is sent to the AI. First pass, not a full secrets scanner.
